The sheet puts the whole retrofit on one tall page, drawn with the 500k values from Lipstick. To print it, use page A (panels 1 to 3) and page B (panels 4 to 8). The sheet uses a **functional contact map** for the switch: it shows what each lever position joins, not where the lugs sit on a particular switch, so find your own switch's lugs with a meter, as in [Identify your lugs](/guitars/relay/customize/treble-bleed/build/what-changes-and-lugs#identify-your-lugs). The text below repeats what the sheet shows, so you can search it.

### Switch contacts

| Mode     | Contact                                                 | Net     |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Direct   | Common to the Direct lug (`SW.C` to `SW.DIRECT`)        | `BUS`   |
| Off      | Common to nothing, the middle rest (`SW.C` to `SW.OFF`) | open    |
| Reactive | Common to the Reactive lug (`SW.C` to `SW.REACTIVE`)    | `REACT` |

`SW.C` is the switch's common lug. `SW.OFF` is the middle lever position: it is not a lug, and it connects to nothing.

### Connection map

Seen from the back, with the shafts pointing away and the lugs at the bottom, the volume pot's left, middle and right lugs are on `BUS`, `OUT` and `GND`. The tone pot's left, middle and right lugs are on `REACT`, `BUS` and `TONE-CAP`.

| Wire       | Connects                                                                                   |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `BUS`      | Pickup selector common, volume pot input end (CW), tone pot wiper, switch Direct lug       |
| `OUT`      | Volume pot wiper, output jack tip, one end of the bleed resistor                           |
| `TB-MID`   | The other end of the bleed resistor, one end of the bleed capacitor                        |
| `TB-SW`    | The other end of the bleed capacitor, switch common                                        |
| `TONE-CAP` | Tone pot CCW end, one end of the 22 nF tone capacitor                                      |
| `REACT`    | Tone pot CW end, switch Reactive lug                                                       |
| `GND`      | Volume pot ground end (CCW), the other end of the 22 nF tone capacitor, output jack sleeve |

If your reused bleed has the capacitor on the wiper, the two parts swap places; the nets are the same.

The rest of the guitar's ground wiring stays as it is. The switch's middle position connects to nothing.

### What each mode does

| Mode     | Where the bleed goes                                                                                             |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Direct   | From the volume wiper (`OUT`) through the resistor and capacitor to the switch common, then to `BUS`.            |
| Off      | The switch common lands on the middle position, which connects to nothing. The bleed is disconnected.            |
| Reactive | From `OUT` through the resistor and capacitor to the switch common, then along `REACT` to the tone pot's CW end. |

In every mode the tone knob works the same way, because the tone pot's wiper stays on BUS and the capacitor stays on its CCW end. At tone 10 the tone wiper reaches the CW end, so Reactive joins BUS directly and matches Direct. At volume 10 the volume wiper reaches the input end, so `OUT` is joined to `BUS` and all three modes are the same.

## Worked example: Relay Lipstick

Relay Lipstick is the guitar I validated this on, and Diagrams [11](/guitars/relay/customize/treble-bleed/build/is-it-a-fit) and [12](/guitars/relay/customize/treble-bleed/build/what-changes-and-lugs) are drawn from its circuit. It has 500k volume and tone pots, so it uses the 500k row of the [value table](/guitars/relay/customize/treble-bleed/build/parts-and-hole#choose-your-bleed-values), and its stock series bleed (a 1 nF capacitor and a 150 kΩ resistor) is reused as it is.

**The lugs, seen from the back.** On the volume pot, the left lug is on BUS, the middle lug is on OUT and the right lug is on ground; none of them change. On the stock tone pot, the left lug is unused, the middle lug goes to the 22 nF capacitor and the right lug is on BUS. After the retrofit, the left lug is on `REACT`, the middle lug is on `BUS` and the right lug is on `TONE-CAP`.

**The bleed.** Lipstick's 150 kΩ resistor sits on the volume wiper and its 1 nF capacitor sits on the volume pot's input lug. Only the capacitor's lead moves: off the input lug and onto the switch common.

**The volume push-push.** Lipstick's volume knob is a push-push switch that adds the lipstick pickup and partially splits the bridge. That switch is a separate set of lugs on the volume pot, and the retrofit doesn't touch it. One of its lugs is wired to BUS; leave it exactly where it is when you add the wire to the Direct lug, and the push-push keeps working as before.

**The hole.** The switch goes near the tone knob, where there is room for the lever and for the switch body inside the control cavity. Choose the exact spot on your own guitar using the checks in [Make room for the switch](/guitars/relay/customize/treble-bleed/build/parts-and-hole#make-room-for-the-switch).

The stock Lipstick circuit, with its pickups and push-push, is on the [Lipstick wiring page](/guitars/relay/wiring?voicing=lipstick).

## Tuning by ear

The values in the [value table](/guitars/relay/customize/treble-bleed/build/parts-and-hole#choose-your-bleed-values) are where every bleed starts, not where every bleed ends. How bright a bleed sounds depends on the pickups (how strong they are and how much treble they have to begin with) and on the cable, since a longer cable drains more highs away. The same parts can sound just right on one guitar and a little bright on another.

Tune in Direct, because it is the plainest bleed and Reactive uses the same parts. A good routine:

1. Pick one volume setting where you most often play, such as 6 or 7, and keep the tone at 10.
2. Listen in Direct, then in Off. Direct should sound clearer than Off without sounding thin or harsh.
3. If Direct sounds too bright or thin, try a smaller capacitor (470–680 pF). If it still sounds dull, try a larger one (1.5–2.2 nF).
4. Once the capacitor is close, move the resistor one standard value up to calm the bleed a little, or down to open it up.
5. Change one part at a time, and listen again at the same settings with the same cable and amp each time.

You can tack a part in with a quick solder joint while you listen and solder it properly once you've chosen. When Direct sounds right, check Reactive with the tone rolled back: it will follow along, because it uses the same parts.

## Troubleshooting

- **The tone knob works backwards** (darkest at 10). The `REACT` wire and the 22 nF capacitor are on swapped tone pot ends. Swap the two end wires.
- **Direct and Reactive sound different at tone 10.** The Reactive wire is on the wrong tone pot lug. Recheck the CW end with the meter ([Diagram 13](/guitars/relay/customize/treble-bleed/build/what-changes-and-lugs#identify-your-lugs)) and move `REACT` there.
- **Reactive sounds just like Direct at every tone setting.** The Reactive wire is on the tone wiper or on BUS, or both throw lugs are joined to BUS. It belongs on the tone pot's CW end.
- **Off still sounds bright, or one mode sounds like another.** The switch common is probably misidentified. Repeat the [switch test](/guitars/relay/customize/treble-bleed/build/what-changes-and-lugs#identify-your-lugs) with the meter and move the wires to match.
- **The modes are in the wrong lever positions.** Swap the wires on the Direct and Reactive lugs.
- **No mode changes the sound as you turn down.** The bleed isn't connected: check that one end is on the volume wiper (`OUT`), the joint between the two parts (`TB-MID`) is solid, and the other end is on the switch common (`TB-SW`).
- **The modes differ at volume 10.** In a correct harness the switch can't change anything at full volume, so something is on the wrong lug or touching ground. Check that the bleed's near end is on the volume wiper (`OUT`) and that no switch lug or bleed lead touches a pot body or the shielding.
- **Pops or crackle when you flip the switch.** Reflow any dull or lumpy solder joints, and check that no stray wire strand or bare lead touches a pot body, the shielding or a neighboring lug.
- **The switch won't sit flat, or the cover won't go back on.** The switch body or its wires are hitting the cavity wall or another part. Turn the switch so its lugs face open space, shorten the wires, or route them around the obstacle.
