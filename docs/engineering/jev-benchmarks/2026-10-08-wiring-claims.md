# Jev vs Claude: wiring-page claims

- **Date:** 2026-10-08
- **Status:** Experimental result. No workflow was changed.
- **Question:** Under the [rule for where Jev belongs](../../decisions/2026-10-01-typesafe-skill.md#where-jev-belongs-2026-10-08), is Jev cheaper, faster, and just as accurate as Claude at checking wiring-page claims against their netlists?

## Method

- **Claims:** the 466 claims that `scripts/typesafe/check-wiring-pages.mjs` extracts from the eight published wiring pages. The pages passed review on 2026-10-03, so each original claim is labeled "no error".
- **Planted errors:** 36 claims, 3 to 6 per page, each a copy of an original prose claim with one realistic mistake: a swapped pickup position, switch state, pot lug, or connection. Claude checked each mutant against its netlist document. None changes a component value or introduces an unknown net label, so the script's exact code checks cannot catch them. The full list is below.
- **Batches:** up to 40 claims per request, the same batches for every model, with the page's whole netlist document as context. No batch holds both a mutant and the original it came from.
- **Jev:** the script's own request format, model `jev-1.13.0`, four requests in parallel.
- **Claude:** Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, and Opus 5.5, run through `claude -p` with no tools, a one-line system prompt, and the same verdict definitions. Three requests in parallel. The cost is the list price that Claude Code reports, including about 4,000 tokens of fixed prompt per call.
- **Scoring:** a planted error counts as caught when the verdict is "contradicts". A false alarm is "contradicts" on an original claim.

## Results

| Checker                 | Planted errors caught | False alarms on 466 originals | Cost    | Median per batch | Wall time |
| ----------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------- | ---------------- | --------- |
| Jev                     | 33 of 36              | 4, all below confidence 0.5   | $0.0073 | 0.18 s           | 1.2 s     |
| Claude Haiku 5.5        | 30 of 36              | 14                            | $0.069  | 6.5 s            | 88 s      |
| Claude Sonnet 5.5       | 32 of 36              | 7                             | $0.74   | 6.8 s            | 78 s      |
| Claude Opus 5.5         | 36 of 36              | 0                             | $1.65   | 10.7 s           | 93 s      |
| Jev, then Opus on flags | 35 of 36              | 0                             | $0.49   | n/a              | 33 s      |

- **Jev's review rule** (a "contradicts" verdict, or any verdict below confidence 0.6) flagged 35 of 36 planted errors and 45 of 466 originals. At priority 1 (a "contradicts" at 0.6 or above) it caught 31 with no false alarms.
- **The one error Jev passed with confidence** is m32 (0.94 "supports"). It is arguably not an error: the tip does reach ground through 500 kΩ of track. Only Opus called it a contradiction. Excluding m32, Jev's review rule flagged every planted error.
- **Jev, then Opus on flags:** Opus re-checked only the 80 claims that Jev's review rule flagged. It confirmed all 35 flagged errors and cleared all 45 flagged originals.

## Conclusion

- **Against Haiku and Sonnet, Jev meets the rule.** It is more accurate (more errors caught, fewer false alarms), about 10 to 100 times cheaper, and about 40 times faster per batch.
- **Against Opus, Jev alone does not.** It is about 225 times cheaper and 60 times faster, but Opus caught every planted error with no false alarms. Jev's review rule recovers the misses only by sending about 10% of claims to review.
- **Jev first, Opus on flags** matched Opus on every unambiguous error at 30% of the cost and a third of the wall time.

## Limits

- 36 planted errors is a small sample. Each count has an uncertainty of a few errors.
- Claude wrote the mutants and labeled them, which may favor Claude. The scoring checks only whether a checker says "contradicts", not whether it says "supports" or "says nothing" correctly.
- Claude costs are list prices for one-shot calls. They do not include an agent session's own context, which would make Claude more expensive in practice.
